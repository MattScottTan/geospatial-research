\begin{abstract}
\noindent
\textbf{[Placeholder abstract --- T-008.]}
When neighbours are defined by a $k$-nearest-neighbour rule, the resulting
neighbour graph can split into two or more pieces that never exchange
information, and no widely used toolkit reports that it has happened. This paper
shows that the split biases the Getis--Ord $\Gistar$ local statistic through a
global-standardisation artifact --- $\Gistar$ measures level, and level is
exactly what a component split displaces --- while leaving Moran's $\MoranI$
essentially unaffected, because $\MoranI$ measures co-variation, which the split
does not displace. Numbers, figures and citations in this draft are placeholders
until the evidence tasks land; every one of them will be traceable through
\texttt{papers/CLAIMS.md}.
\end{abstract}

\section{The mechanism\CONTRIBUTION}
\label{sec:mechanism}

\textbf{[Placeholder --- T-008.]} The theoretical core: $\Gistar$ compares a
neighbourhood's sum against what it would be if the neighbourhood held the
\emph{global} mean, standardised by the \emph{global} standard deviation, so it
measures level. Moran's $\MoranI$ measures co-variation. A component split
displaces level, not co-variation.

\begin{keyinsight}
The bias is not in $\Gistar$'s arithmetic; it is in the mismatch between a
\emph{local} neighbourhood and a \emph{global} standardisation constant, which a
disconnected graph makes untenable.
\end{keyinsight}
