# Run 6 v2 Exemplar Style Takeaways

Source used as structural guideline: `Cloudy with a Chance of Compute.pdf`.

1. **Begin with a concrete contrast.** The exemplar opens with Singapore near cloud compute and Lagos far from it, making the spatial problem legible before introducing methods. Culinary Corridors should begin with a contrast such as Thai-Vietnamese mainland proximity, Chinese-Korean regional linkage, and Filipino island/maritime links.

2. **Move from a hook into a research problem.** The exemplar does not start with methods. It first explains why the problem matters, then introduces the central question. Culinary Corridors should first explain why cuisine similarity needs geography, then state the residual-corridor question.

3. **Create an atlas-method scaffold.** The exemplar's “How the atlas works” section lays out three stages: comparison frame, infrastructure/access measurement, and activity overlay/testing. Culinary Corridors should use an analogous four-stage scaffold: cuisine profiles, similarity, geography, and residual corridors.

4. **Use numbered findings.** The exemplar gives each analytical result a named finding and explains what the figure shows, why the method matters, and what the result means. Culinary Corridors should convert its figures into findings, not merely insert maps.

5. **Explain method choices in plain language.** The exemplar explains why tests such as Moran's I and Gi* were chosen. Culinary Corridors should explain why cosine similarity, distance baselines, residuals, focused cases, and bridge scores were used.

6. **Distinguish visual suggestion from spatial evidence.** The exemplar distinguishes maps that reveal patterns from statistical tests that support structure. Culinary Corridors should distinguish ingredient similarity from distance-adjusted residual geography.

7. **Move from global patterns to focused cases.** The exemplar uses global results, then city cases. Culinary Corridors should use the global screen as discovery, then move to East/Southeast Asia and cuisine-pair vignettes.

8. **Make case studies do analytical work.** The exemplar's Singapore, Dublin, Ho Chi Minh City, and Lagos sections each represent a different relationship between distance, infrastructure, and activity. Culinary Corridors should use Thai-Vietnamese, Chinese-Korean, and Filipino links to represent different spatial situations.

9. **Keep limitations close to the result they qualify.** The exemplar repeatedly states what a metric does and does not show. Culinary Corridors should caveat the recipe corpus, cuisine labels, residuals, and topographic map near the relevant sections.

10. **End with sources and reproducibility.** The exemplar closes with data sources, tools, and bibliography. Culinary Corridors should include source families, internal artifact families, tools, and PDF backup notes.

11. **Let visuals lead, but do not let visuals stand alone.** The exemplar provides explanatory paragraphs after each figure. Culinary Corridors should provide 300–700 words of explanation around each major figure.

12. **Use a public-facing but technical tone.** The exemplar is not a traditional paper, but it includes enough method detail for evaluation. Culinary Corridors should be readable as a StoryMap while still showing analytical rigor.
