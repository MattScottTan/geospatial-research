# Run 2 v2 Scope-Lock Decision Memo

Created: 2026-04-29

## Decision

The final project should proceed as:

> **Global discovery screen + East/Southeast Asia primary case + Iberian/Atlantic secondary/diagnostic case.**

The global 20-cuisine model remains useful for showing that cuisine similarity is spatially structured and for discovering candidate residual corridors. However, final inferential weight should rest on scoped cases where the spatial logic is clearer and where the project can avoid claiming that a coarse global recipe platform explains all culinary geography.

## Global discovery layer

- Retain all **20 cuisines** and **39,774 recipes** for the opening similarity heatmap, distance-decay plot, and residual-corridor screen.
- Interpret global residuals as **candidate corridors**, not as proof of migration, trade, or colonial causation.
- Use the global layer to motivate focused analysis.

## Primary focused case: East/Southeast Asia

Available cuisines: chinese, filipino, japanese, korean, thai, vietnamese.

This case is strong because it includes **6 of 6 target cuisines**, enough for **15 dyads**, and it supports a meaningful spatial question: whether cuisine similarity follows subregional adjacency, distance, and land/sea corridor structure more than a generic global distance model suggests.

Permitted conclusion if Run 2 v2 outputs support it:

> In the East/Southeast Asian subset, cuisine similarity appears spatially organized around regional adjacency and cross-subregional links, but the prototype does not prove a specific historical mechanism.

## Secondary focused case: Iberian/Atlantic-Pacific corridor

Available cuisines: brazilian, cajun_creole, filipino, jamaican, mexican, southern_us, spanish.

This case is retained as a **secondary or diagnostic** case because it includes **7 of 7 target cuisines** and visually illustrates long-distance residual-corridor logic. It is more exposed to recipe-platform bias, colonial-history overinterpretation, and broad cuisine labels, so it should not carry the strongest final claim by itself.

Permitted conclusion if supported:

> Some long-distance residual similarities are consistent with maritime/colonial/diasporic corridor hypotheses, but the prototype treats them as hypotheses requiring stronger covariate modeling.

## Diagnostic/excluded cases

No cuisine is excluded from the global screen. The Anglo-American/Southern U.S. residual family is treated as **diagnostic**, not as the main finding, because top residuals involving British, Irish, Russian, and Southern U.S. cuisines may be amplified by English-language recipe-platform bias and generic pantry/baking ingredients.

## Strong claims allowed

- Cuisine similarity is not random with respect to geography.
- A distance-only model leaves structured residuals.
- Generic-ingredient filtering changes which corridors look strongest, which is important evidence about data quality.
- Focused cases support stronger interpretation than the global screen.

## Cautious hypotheses allowed

- Some residual corridors may reflect migration, trade, colonial ties, maritime exchange, or shared agricultural/ingredient histories.
- East/Southeast Asian similarities may be better interpreted through regional adjacency and land/sea connectivity than through straight-line distance alone.
- Iberian/Atlantic-Pacific links may reflect historical exchange, but also may reflect recipe-platform vocabulary bias.

## Forbidden final claims

- Do not claim that migration, trade, or colonialism caused any pairwise similarity unless Run 3 adds explicit covariates and robustness checks.
- Do not claim this corpus represents all world cuisines.
- Do not treat cuisine labels as exact nation-states.
- Do not treat the global residual map as a final explanatory model.
