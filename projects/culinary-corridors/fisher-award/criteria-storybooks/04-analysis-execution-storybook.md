# Analytical Approach and Execution Storybook

## Criterion definition

This criterion rewards methodological seriousness: the project should show that the chosen geospatial approach was not only interesting in theory but executed in a way that produces defensible conclusions. The strongest accessible winners reveal comparison logic, classification logic, mechanism testing, or explicit limits/accuracy statements.[P1][P2][P3][P5][P8]

## Evaluation signals

- The artifact describes a clear analytical procedure rather than only a map outcome.[P1][P3][P5][P8]
- The method matches the question instead of looking chosen for novelty alone.[P2][P3][P5]
- The project reports some form of validation, mechanism, comparison, or explicit limitation where appropriate.[P3][P5][P8]
- The maps are tied to an inferential step: ranking, classification, detection, pre/post comparison, or index construction.[P1][P2][P3][P5][P8]
- The work feels finished rather than merely exploratory.[P2][P3][P5][P8]

## Winner case studies

### Top-tier accessible cases

- **Dev Patel (5/5 in the current matrix):** Dev is the strongest accessible analysis case because the project does not stop at flood measurement. It links measurement to economic effects, adaptation, and explicit mechanism testing, which gives the work real analytical depth.[P3]
- **Bora Ju (5/5):** Bora’s project is a model of visible execution. The public project page names the supervised CART classifier, documents the imagery source, reports 83% overall accuracy, and acknowledges a concrete limitation tied to spatial resolution.[P5]

### Strong 4/5 cases

- **Shane Rice (4/5):** Shane’s work clearly analyzes settlement destruction rather than merely mapping place, but the current public snippet does not expose enough validation detail to justify a top score. `uncertain-inference`.[P2]
- **Aanchal Chopra (4/5):** Aanchal’s project presents a methodology for integrating extreme heat into walkability indexes. That makes the work analytical rather than purely descriptive, even though the public evidence is thinner than Dev’s or Bora’s. `uncertain-inference`.[P1]
- **Beatrice Youd (4/5):** Beatrice’s project asks two comparative impact questions — about forest loss and accessibility — which supports a solid analysis score even if the public artifact does not expose the full technical pipeline. `uncertain-inference`.[P4]
- **Emilio Sempris (4/5):** Emilio’s work goes beyond visual display by processing fire data to identify the top 100 affected jurisdictions. The execution is clear and consequential, though analytically simpler than the strongest model- or classification-heavy cases.[P8]

## Anti-patterns and omissions

- Descriptive mapping without an explicit analytical step will rarely read as Fisher-level execution.
- If the artifact hides the procedure, readers cannot distinguish between an insightful method and a compelling picture. That is one reason several public artifacts remain `partial` despite having public titles or StoryMaps.[S7][P6][S1][P7]
- A future entrant should avoid assuming that technical sophistication is self-evident. If the method is not legible in the artifact, it may as well not exist for judging purposes.[P1][P2][P3][P5]

## Win tactics

- State the analytical workflow in plain language.
- Show why the chosen method fits the question better than a simpler alternative.
- Include a quality-control signal where possible: accuracy, limitation, sensitivity, comparison, or mechanism test.[P3][P5]
- Make sure the final artifact shows at least one inferential step, not just the final map.
- If the project is exploratory, convert that exploration into a clearly stated finding before submission.[P8]

## Future-submission checklist

- Is the method visible, not just the result?
- Does the analytical design fit the substantive question?
- Is there evidence of validation, limitation, or disciplined comparison?
- Would a judge understand the execution logic without reading a full paper?
- Does the artifact distinguish analysis from descriptive display?
