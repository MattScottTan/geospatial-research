# Causal Boundary and Takeaways

Date: 2026-03-14 12:01 PM America/New_York

## Final-package language

### What the project can say confidently
- Major cloud infrastructure is geographically uneven.
- AI-linked cities occupy a much more compute-proximate part of the global city system than the broader 8,000-city frame.
- That pattern survives descriptive, spatial-diagnostic, and spatial-model scrutiny strongly enough to justify treating compute access as a meaningful correlate and screening layer.

### What the project should not claim
- It should **not** claim that proximity to cloud regions universally causes city-level AI growth.
- It should **not** claim that every city far from compute is excluded from AI.
- It should **not** claim that one significant fixed-effects result settles the causal question.

## Interesting takeaways that remain worth keeping

1. **The non-result is informative.**
   The project tried to push toward causality and found instability rather than a clean treatment effect. That makes the final submission more credible, not less.

2. **Compute appears to work as part of a bundle.**
   The mixed causal story is consistent with a richer mechanism in which connectivity, institutions, power, and market structure mediate whether compute proximity translates into visible AI activity.

3. **Outliers matter more, not less.**
   Because the causal story is not uniform, the outlier cities become more important. They help explain why some places align with the broad pattern and others depart from it.

## Recommended placement in the final submission

- **Main StoryMap:** one or two restrained sentences in the short-answer / limits sections.
- **Main report:** a compact paragraph in methods/limits plus a slightly fuller discussion in the limits or appendix guide.
- **Supplemental materials:** keep the stage-specific causal summaries available for technical readers.
