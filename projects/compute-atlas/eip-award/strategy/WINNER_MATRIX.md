# WINNER_MATRIX.md — EIP + Fisher Prize Winner Evidence Matrix

## Scope Statement

This matrix inventories **past winners** of the following Harvard Center for Geographic Analysis (CGA) prizes:

1. **EIP Student of the Year Award** — recognizes exceptional student geospatial projects submitted through the Esri Innovation Program (EIP) at Harvard.
2. **Fisher Prize** — recognizes outstanding student work in geographic information science, typically split into undergraduate and graduate categories.

The matrix also includes a small **benchmark set** of official Esri StoryMap exemplars or competition winners *only if* they are needed to fill gaps in publicly available Harvard winner artifacts.

## Inclusion Rules

| Rule | Detail |
|------|--------|
| **In scope** | Any EIP Student of the Year or Fisher Prize winner listed on official Harvard CGA pages, regardless of year. |
| **In scope** | The public StoryMap, project page, poster, or report directly linked from an official winner listing. |
| **In scope (benchmark)** | Up to 3 official Esri StoryMap competition winners or exemplars, used only if fewer than 6 Harvard winner artifacts are publicly accessible. |
| **Out of scope** | Non-geospatial Harvard prizes, discipline-specific thesis prizes, non-Harvard GIS competitions (unless benchmark). |
| **Out of scope** | Unpublished student work, drafts, or artifacts behind authentication walls (recorded as BLOCKED). |

### ID Convention
- EIP winners: `[EIP-YYYY-#]` (e.g., `[EIP-2023-1]`)
- Fisher undergraduate winners: `[FIS-YYYY-UG-#]`
- Fisher graduate winners: `[FIS-YYYY-G-#]`
- Benchmark exemplars: `[BM-YYYY-#]`

## Criteria Capture

### A. EIP Student of the Year — Official Judging Criteria
**Source:** [CGA EIP Award Page](https://gis.harvard.edu/event/edc-student-year-award)

The EIP award recognizes "outstanding achievements in developing novel tools or techniques for geographic analysis using Esri technology." Submissions must be an ArcGIS StoryMap showcasing the project.

**Official judging criteria (verbatim from CGA page):**
- Potential impact
- Innovation
- Originality
- Implementation
- Organization

**Submission requirements:**
- ArcGIS StoryMap format, shared publicly
- Must include: short bio + professional picture, brief description of achievement, project description, Esri technology used, maps/narrative/apps/video/graphics/charts
- Must use Esri technology

### B. Fisher Prize — Official Selection Criteria
**Source:** [CGA Fisher Prize Page](https://gis.harvard.edu/event/fisher-prize-award-competition)

The Fisher Prize recognizes "excellence in GIS" for one undergraduate and one graduate Harvard student. Submissions may be a poster (42"×36" PDF, ≤350 words) or a StoryMap URL.

**Official selection criteria (verbatim from CGA page):**
- Innovation and creativity of chosen topic
- Use of GIS in performing the project
- Data — complexity, relevance to topic, properly documented
- Analytical approach and execution
- Visualization / cartographic communication effectiveness

### C. Esri ArcGIS StoryMaps Competition — Official Judging Criteria (benchmark reference)
**Source:** [Esri Blog, Nov 2025](https://www.esri.com/arcgis-blog/products/story-maps/constituent-engagement/2025-arcgis-storymaps-competition-criteria)

Three broad criteria provided to judges:
- **Overall design, impact, and originality** — well-written, well-organized, creative approach, strong visual language / custom theme, passion and accessibility for non-expert audience
- **Innovative use of GIS and the ArcGIS StoryMaps product** — geospatial element used as narrative-building tool, demonstration of builder knowledge (sidecars, express maps, embedded apps), purposeful block usage rather than "button-mashing"
- **Support of the competition and category theme** — clear approach to what the submission is trying to accomplish, major takeaway or call to action

**Additional judging advice from Esri (paraphrased):**
- Save deep GIS integration for later in the story after context is established
- Use custom themes to create unified visual identity
- Unorthodox narrative approaches (e.g., first-person non-human narrator) can stand out
- Living Atlas data integration is required and valued
