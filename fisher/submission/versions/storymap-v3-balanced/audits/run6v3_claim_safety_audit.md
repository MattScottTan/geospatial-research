# Run 6 v3 Claim Safety Audit

## Result
The revised StoryMap text preserves the approved claim hierarchy and specifically corrects the prior risk that the presentation could appear too Asia-driven.

## Strong claims retained
- Cuisine similarity is spatially structured.
- Distance explains part, but not all, of cuisine similarity.
- Positive residuals identify candidate culinary corridors.
- East/Southeast Asia is the strongest focused inference case.
- Residual bridge scores require GIS/spatial residuals and produce a geospatial-only insight.
- The Run 5 relief map makes the East/Southeast Asia corridor more spatially legible.

## Cautious claims retained
- Iberian/Atlantic-Pacific residuals are suggestive and diagnostic.
- Non-Asia long-distance residuals are hypothesis-generating.
- Selected patterns are consistent with corridor plausibility, regional adjacency, and coastal/maritime context, but not proof of those mechanisms.
- Europe/Atlantic-linked material is useful for future focused analysis but not treated as a full primary case.

## Unsafe claims checked and avoided
- No claim that migration, trade, colonialism, empire, topography, or maritime routes caused the observed similarity.
- No claim that the recipe corpus represents all world cuisines.
- No claim that cuisine labels map cleanly to nation-states.
- No claim that residual links are historical routes.
- No claim that Europe has been fully analyzed as a focused regional case.
- No claim that the relief map is a least-cost or causal terrain model.

## Regional-balance edits verified
- The global discovery section now explains that the project starts globally and selects East/Southeast Asia from that screen.
- The Asia section is renamed as a methodological narrowing: “From global screen to focused case.”
- The Iberian/Atlantic-Pacific section is mandatory and explicitly non-Asia diagnostic.
- A dedicated note explains why Europe/Atlantic-linked material remains diagnostic rather than primary.
