# Area 1 — Payoff Visibility

## Why this matters for scoring
The packet's tie-breaker #4 — "actionable or interpretive payoff wins" — is the dimension where this project loses to a comparable submission. The current conclusion lands on a methodological payoff ("the shape is invisible in any analysis that treats distance as the only spatial variable"), which is true but inward-facing. The rubric rewards outward payoffs: what does the analysis enable someone to do, ask, or see that they couldn't before. The Filipino HL signature → Manila Galleon → trade-record-testable hypothesis chain is already implicit in the Filipino portrait. It needs to be elevated to the conclusion so the judge sees it without having to assemble it.

## Tasks

### 1.1 — Add a hypothesis-generating payoff paragraph before the closing pull-quote
- **Where:** "Conclusion" section, immediately before the final blockquote that begins *"Cuisine, viewed through residuals, has a network shape…"*
- **Insert:** A single paragraph naming the Manila Galleon connection as the cleanest example of a testable historical hypothesis the residual network produces, then generalizing to the Russian and Spanish nodes as comparable testable corridors.
- **Done when:** The paragraph (a) names at least one specific historical mechanism (Manila Galleon, 1565–1815) the residual fingerprint corresponds to, (b) frames the residual network as hypothesis-generating rather than conclusive, and (c) explicitly says these residuals are testable against trade, migration, or colonization records.
- **Length cap:** 90–120 words. Do not exceed.
- **Tone check:** Hypothesis-generating, not hypothesis-confirming. Avoid "proves," "demonstrates," "shows that." Use "is the cartographic fingerprint of," "is consistent with," "is testable against."

### 1.2 — Surface the payoff in the introduction
- **Where:** Introduction section, the paragraph beginning "Most analyses of food treat cuisine as a cultural object…"
- **Add:** One sentence after that paragraph announcing that the project's residual structure produces specific, testable historical-exchange hypotheses — not just methodological commentary.
- **Done when:** A judge reading only the introduction can name at least one concrete deliverable (testable historical corridors) the analysis produces.
- **Length cap:** One sentence, ≤30 words.
- **Risk to avoid:** Do not promise findings the body does not deliver. The body delivers structural geography and named bridge cuisines; it does not deliver verified historical claims. Frame accordingly.

### 1.3 — Audit the three audience callouts in the existing conclusion
- **Where:** Conclusion paragraph that names "food-systems researchers, agricultural economists, and historians of exchange," and "methodologists," and "curious general readers."
- **Tighten:** The food-systems-researchers sentence currently asks "which historical events, ecological conditions, or trade networks correspond with which residual links?" — make this sentence concrete by naming the Manila Galleon, Iberian colonial network, and trans-polar Russian–Atlantic exchange as the three corridors the residual structure most clearly identifies for follow-up inquiry.
- **Done when:** The food-systems sentence names specific historical exchange networks, not generic "historical events."
- **Risk to avoid:** Do not replace caution with overreach. The sentence still reads as "starting point for inquiry," not "proof of."

### 1.4 — Verify payoff alignment across intro, body, and conclusion
- **Pass once through the document checking:** intro flags hypothesis-generating payoff; body (Filipino, Russian, Spanish portraits) supplies the mechanism; conclusion delivers the named corridors.
- **Done when:** A judge skim-reading only the intro, the four cuisine portraits, and the conclusion gets a coherent payoff arc without contradiction.
- **Failure mode to catch:** Do not let the intro promise more than the body proves. If the intro says "testable corridors," the conclusion must say "testable corridors," not "proven exchange routes."

# Area 2 — Framing

## Why this matters for scoring
Two distinct framing problems, both visible to a 30-second judge scan:

**Problem A: the title is a label, not a claim.** The playbook is direct on this — "Use a claim-based title: 'Flood risk concentrates where recovery capacity is weakest,' not 'Flood Map.'" The current title ("Bridges Across Cuisines: Mapping the residual geography…") describes the activity. It does not announce the conclusion. A claim-title moves the project from descriptive ("here is some analysis") to argumentative ("here is what the analysis shows"), which is the framing past Fisher winners share.

**Problem B: the central question is buried.** The current introduction spends ~280 words on three example pairs before the central research question appears. In long-form prose this is good writing. In StoryMap scroll-format with a 30-second judge comprehension window, it is too slow. The packet's StoryMap structure recommends: hook → spatial question → data/method → maps → synthesis → limits. The central question should land before the third scroll, not after.

## Tasks

### 2.1 — Replace the title with a claim-title pair
- **Where:** Document title (top of page 1) and subtitle.
- **New title:** *Cuisine resemblance has a shape that distance can't predict*
- **New subtitle:** *Mapping the residual network that connects archipelagos, peninsulas, and Atlantic shores*
- **Done when:** The title is a sentence that asserts a finding; the subtitle names the analytical object (residual network) and the structural geographies (archipelagos, peninsulas, Atlantic shores).
- **Why this title specifically:**
  - "Cuisine resemblance" is the dependent variable, named directly.
  - "Has a shape" is the project's core empirical claim — defensible, since R² = 0.40 means 60% of variation is structured but unexplained by distance.
  - "Distance can't predict" specifies what the shape is residual *to* — this is the project's analytical hinge.
  - Subtitle names the three structural geographies (archipelagic, peninsular, Atlantic) the bridge analysis identifies, so the judge knows the body's structure before scrolling.
- **Alternative if this lands wrong on re-read:** "After distance, cuisines connect through archipelagos — not straight lines." Use only if the recommended title feels too declarative for the work.
- **Risk to avoid:** Do not replace "Bridges Across Cuisines" with another label. "Mapping X," "Exploring Y," "Investigating Z" are all labels. The title must contain a verb that asserts.

### 2.2 — Front-load the central question in the introduction
- **Where:** Introduction section, paragraphs 1–4.
- **Restructure:** Move the central research question (currently in paragraph four, beginning "The central question is:") into paragraph two, immediately after the "geography of straight-line kilometers" sentence.
- **Move the three example pairs (Thai–Vietnamese, British–Russian, Filipino–Brazilian) to the position currently occupied by paragraphs three and four, framed as evidence for the question rather than buildup to it.**
- **Done when:** A reader reaching the bottom of the first scroll-screen has already encountered (a) the puzzle ("a striking fraction of the variation… cannot be explained by geographic distance"), (b) the central research question (the indented blockquote), and (c) the hero map. Examples follow.
- **Word budget:** No net additions. This is a reordering, not a rewrite.
- **Failure mode to catch:** Do not delete the three example pairs. They are the project's strongest concrete hook. Just move them.

### 2.3 — Tighten the hero map caption to a claim
- **Where:** Figure 1 caption.
- **Current state:** The caption reads "Candidate residual cuisine corridors across the project corpus. Blue great-circle links mark the East/Southeast Asia focused case…"
- **Replace with a claim-led caption:** Open the caption with one sentence stating what the map *shows* (cuisines connected by ingredient resemblance distance cannot explain), then move the link-color and corpus-coverage notes to follow as supporting detail.
- **Done when:** The first sentence of the caption is an argument, not a label.
- **Length cap:** Caption stays at or below current length. This is a reordering and tightening, not an expansion.
- **Risk to avoid:** Keep the corpus-coverage note (the disclaimer about Africa, South Asia, the Middle East, and Oceania being absent because the corpus does not contain enough labeled cuisines there). That disclaimer is one of the project's strongest rigor signals and protects against an obvious judge objection.

### 2.4 — Audit Findings 2, 3, and 4 captions for claim-vs-label phrasing
- **Where:** Captions for Figures 4, 5, 6, and 7.
- **Pass criterion:** Each caption's first sentence should state what the figure *shows* (the finding), not what the figure *is* (the chart type).
- **Examples of the difference:**
  - Label phrasing: "Mean residual cuisine similarity by spatial grouping."
  - Claim phrasing: "Long-distance Iberian–Atlantic–Pacific pairs are more similar than distance predicts by a wider margin than even regional neighbors."
- **Done when:** All four captions open with a claim sentence; all four still contain the methodological detail (n, weights, what the colors mean) further into the caption.
- **Length:** Net-neutral. Claim sentences replace existing first sentences; existing detail moves to second-and-later sentences.
- **Failure mode to catch:** Do not strip methodological detail from captions. The packet rewards "captions as micro-arguments" but also "scale, legend, source clarity." Both have to coexist.

### 2.5 — Verify "Stage 1 / Stage 2 / Stage 3" framing in How the Analysis Works survives the front-loading change
- **Where:** "How the analysis works" section.
- **Pass criterion:** After the introduction is restructured (Task 2.2), the Stage 1/2/3 explanation should still feel like a natural next step, not a redundant re-framing.
- **Done when:** The transition from intro (now ending on "the latter are where this project's analytical interest lies" plus the new front-loaded question) into Stage 1 reads cleanly without orphan sentences.
- **Risk to avoid:** Do not delete or rewrite Stage 1/2/3. The methodology section is doing scoring work on the Use-of-GIS and Analytical-Approach criteria. Just confirm the seam holds.

# Area 3 — Cartographic First Impression

## Why this matters for scoring
The visualization criterion is the project's lowest weighted score (4/5, 13.6 weighted points). The figures themselves are good — the issue is the *first impression* a judge gets in the 10-second and 30-second scan windows the packet specifies. Three specific cartographic-first-impression risks are addressable without re-rendering anything.

## Tasks

### 3.1 — Verify the hero map is the first visual block in the StoryMap
- **Where:** StoryMap page sequence, top of document.
- **Pass criterion:** When pasted into ArcGIS StoryMaps, the order of blocks at the top should be: title (claim-title from Task 2.1) → author byline → puzzle paragraph → central question (blockquote) → hero map → examples paragraph → "How the analysis works" section.

- **Failure mode to catch:** ArcGIS StoryMaps sometimes inserts the author byline as a large block that pushes the hero map below the fold. If this happens, demote the byline to a smaller text block and verify on incognito test.

### 3.2 — Verify Moran scatterplot legibility at scroll-speed
- **Where:** Figure 3, right panel.
- **Pass criterion:** The Moran scatterplot's quadrant labels (LH, HL, LL, HH), point labels, and the lower inset reporting Mantel test results must remain legible at default StoryMap rendering size — without zoom, without click-to-expand.
- **Done when:** On a 13-inch laptop screen and on a phone screen in landscape, the four quadrant labels are readable, the named cuisine labels (Filipino, Russian, Mexican, Cajun-Creole, Jamaican, Brazilian) are readable, and the Mantel inset numbers are readable.
- **What to do if it fails legibility:** Do *not* re-render the figure. Instead, in the StoryMap editor, set the figure to render at full content-width rather than half-width, and add a one-sentence caption fragment ("Quadrant abbreviations: HH = high-high cluster, HL = high-low isolated bridge, LH = low-high, LL = low-low.") so the judge does not need to read the in-figure labels to follow the argument.
- **Risk to avoid:** Click-to-expand is not a substitute for legibility. The packet's 30-second comprehension test assumes no click.

### 3.3 — Verify Figure 7 (relief map) does not overwhelm Figure 6 (focused-case map)
- **Where:** Finding 4 figure pair.
- **Pass criterion:** The relief map (Figure 7) should read as elaboration of Figure 6, not as a replacement. A judge skimming should be able to grasp the East/Southeast Asia corridor finding from Figure 6 alone.
- **Done when:** Figure 6 is presented first, has its own complete caption stating the finding, and Figure 7 is presented second with a caption opening on the *additional* information the relief context adds (Tibetan barrier, archipelagic ring, South China Sea as connector).
- **Risk to avoid:** If the StoryMap editor places these two maps too close together, the second one will visually dominate. Use a paragraph-break or scroll-section between them.

### 3.4 — Audit color-legend consistency across the four cuisine-portrait figures
- **Where:** Figures 8, 9, 10, 11 (Filipino, Russian, Thai, Spanish).
- **Pass criterion:** Each portrait should use the same conventions: blue anchor for the focal cuisine, orange or warm-toned partner connections, line width proportional to residual strength.
- **Done when:** A judge can move from Figure 8 to Figure 11 without re-learning the color encoding.
- **What to do if a portrait is inconsistent:** Note the inconsistency in the caption rather than re-rendering. Example: "Color encoding follows Figures 8–10."
- **Risk to avoid:** Do not re-render any of the four portraits. Time-cost is too high relative to scoring gain.

### 3.5 — Verify the "Reading the map" side panels in Figures 6 and 7 are not cropped on mobile
- **Where:** Figures 6 and 7.
- **Pass criterion:** On a phone screen, the side panel listing top residual links and link-type encoding should remain visible alongside the map, not truncated.
- **Done when:** A mobile-browser test confirms the side panel renders.
- **What to do if it fails:** In the StoryMap editor, set the figure to display at full-bleed width (the side panel is part of the rendered image, so it stays attached). Do not split the figure.

### 3.6 — Verify the corpus-coverage disclaimer in Figure 1 is legible

- **Pass criterion:** The disclaimer text noting that Africa, most of South Asia, the Middle East, and Oceania are absent because the corpus does not contain enough labeled cuisines there must remain readable at default rendering size.
