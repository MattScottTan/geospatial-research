# Run 3 Fisher Positioning Memo

Created: 2026-04-29

## Why this project fits the Fisher Prize

The Fisher Prize rewards excellence in Geographic Information Science / GIS. Harvard Center for Geographic Analysis sources describe the Howard T. Fisher Prize as a student GIS award, and public award pages emphasize GIS use, data, analysis, presentation, and innovation. Sources: https://gis.harvard.edu/awards, https://gis.harvard.edu/event/fisher-prize-award-competition, and https://gis.harvard.edu/news/harvard-student-gis-awards.

The project is Fisher-relevant because its central claim is spatial. It does not merely place food data on a map. It uses GIS-style reasoning to ask whether cuisine similarity follows distance, corridors, bridges, or boundaries.

## Spatial necessity

The main insight requires spatial modeling:

- Cuisine similarity is computed from ingredient profiles.
- Geographic distance establishes a spatial baseline.
- Residuals identify cuisine pairs that are more similar than distance predicts.
- Corridor maps show where those residuals are geographically located.
- Residual bridge scores aggregate pairwise residuals into mapped place-level roles.
- Boundary/permeability summaries test whether residuals differ by spatial grouping.

A non-spatial food clustering project could identify similar cuisines, but it could not identify which similarities are unexpected relative to geography, which cuisines act as spatial bridges, or where boundary patterns appear.

## Interdisciplinary value

The project connects:

- **GIS and spatial modeling:** distance baselines, residual corridor mapping, coordinates, regional grouping, and bridge-score mapping.
- **Food science:** ingredient representation, generic-ingredient filtering, and potential future flavor chemistry.
- **Cultural geography:** cuisine similarity as evidence of regional structure and possible historical exchange.
- **Data ethics:** platform bias, cuisine-label uncertainty, and cautious interpretation of mechanisms.

## Visual clarity

The final figure set is designed as a Fisher-facing spatial argument:

1. Global discovery figure: candidate residual corridors.

3. East/Southeast Asia focused case: strongest scoped spatial interpretation.
4. Residual bridge map: geospatial-only insight.
5. Iberian/Atlantic-Pacific secondary case: corridor hypothesis and cautionary contrast.

## Credibility strategy

The project avoids overreach by separating three levels of interpretation:

- **Strong:** spatial structure, residuals, focused case patterns, and bridge-score roles.
- **Cautious:** migration/trade/colonial/maritime hypotheses.
- **Forbidden:** causal proof, corpus representativeness, and exact cuisine-to-nation equivalence.

This claim discipline helps the project remain ambitious while defensible.

## Award-facing positioning sentence

**Culinary Corridors uses GIS to turn food similarity into a spatial question: where does cuisine follow distance, where does it break distance, and which places become bridges in the resulting residual geography?**
