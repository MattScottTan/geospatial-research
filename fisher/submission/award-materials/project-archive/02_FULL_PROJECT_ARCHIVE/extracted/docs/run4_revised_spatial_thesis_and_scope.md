# Run 4 Revised Spatial Thesis and Scope

Created: 2026-04-29

## Recommended title
**Culinary Corridors: Mapping Where Food Similarity Breaks Distance**

## One-sentence spatial thesis
**I use GIS to measure where cuisine similarity follows geographic distance, where it exceeds distance-based expectation, and which places become bridge nodes in the residual geography of food.**

## Required one-sentence Fisher test
I use GIS to show **residual culinary corridors and bridge regions** across a global recipe corpus and focused East/Southeast Asian case, revealing **spatial structure in food similarity that cannot be identified from ingredient clustering alone**.

## Final scope
- **Global discovery screen:** all 20 cuisines from the prototype dataset, used to detect candidate residual corridors.
- **Primary focused inference:** East/Southeast Asia, because its retained cuisines provide the clearest spatially coherent case.
- **Secondary/diagnostic case:** Iberian/Atlantic-Pacific links, used as hypothesis-generating and data-bias-aware evidence.
- **Geospatial-only insight:** bridge-score and boundary/permeability summaries, used to show that residual similarity has place-based spatial roles.

## Strong conclusions
- Cuisine similarity in the prototype is spatially structured.
- Straight-line distance explains part of cuisine similarity but leaves meaningful residuals.
- East/Southeast Asia provides the strongest scoped spatial inference.
- Residual bridge scores convert pairwise food similarity into mapped place-level roles.
- GIS is the engine of the finding, not display.

## Cautious hypotheses
- Some positive residual corridors may be consistent with migration, trade, colonial, maritime, or agricultural exchange histories.
- East/Southeast Asian residual similarities may reflect regional adjacency, food technologies, ingredient ecology, and land/sea exchange.
- Iberian/Atlantic-Pacific residuals may reflect long-distance exchange, but they are more vulnerable to platform and label bias.

## Forbidden claims
- The corpus represents all world cuisines.
- Residual corridors prove causation by migration, trade, empire, colonialism, or maritime networks.
- Cuisine labels are exact nation-state units.
- Global residual maps are causal models.
- Flavor chemistry or fermentation is central without new validation.

## Why GIS is required
A non-spatial analysis can cluster cuisines by ingredients, but it cannot answer which similarities are unexpected relative to distance, where those residuals are located, whether they form focused regional structures, or which places act as bridge nodes in a residual spatial network.
