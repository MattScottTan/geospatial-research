## Figure 2. Method/model figure
**File:** `figures/final_revised/run4_method_or_model_figure.png`

Cuisine similarity plotted against log geographic distance. The fitted trend is the distance baseline. Points above the baseline have positive residuals and become candidate culinary corridors. This makes the GIS operation visible: the project compares similarity to spatial expectation.

## Figure 3. Primary focused case
**File:** `figures/final_revised/run4_primary_case_figure.png`

East/Southeast Asia focused case using filtered residual cuisine links. Thicker blue links indicate stronger positive residual similarity after the distance baseline. This is the strongest inference case because it is spatially coherent and less dependent on broad global interpretation.

## Figure 4. Geospatial insight figure
**File:** `figures/final_revised/run4_geospatial_insight_figure.png`

Residual bridge index by cuisine. The index aggregates positive residual degree, top-residual participation, mean positive residual strength, long-distance residual score, and mean residual into a mapped place-level role. This cannot be obtained from ingredient vectors alone because it depends on the distance baseline and spatial residual network.

## Figure 5. Secondary/limitations figure
**File:** `figures/final_revised/run4_secondary_or_limitations_figure.png`

Boundary/permeability diagnostic summarizing mean residual similarity by spatial grouping. Positive values indicate that similarity is higher than distance-only expectation on average. The result supports the idea that culinary residuals have spatial structure, while remaining diagnostic rather than causal.
