# Final Figure Captions

Created: 2026-04-29

## Figure 1 — `figures/final/final_global_discovery_figure.png`

**Global discovery: filtered residual culinary corridors.** Lines connect cuisine pairs whose observed ingredient-profile similarity is higher than a distance-only model predicts after generic-ingredient filtering. The figure uses the Run 2 v2 filtered residual output and cuisine coordinates. It is a discovery screen: it identifies candidate corridors for interpretation, but it does not prove migration, trade, colonial, or maritime causation.

## Figure 2 — `figures/final/final_distance_or_residual_model_figure.png`

**Distance baseline and residual logic.** This figure shows how cuisine similarity relates to geographic distance. The key inference is not that distance explains everything; rather, distance explains part of the pattern and leaves residuals that can be mapped as candidate culinary corridors. The Run 2 v2 filtered model reported a negative log-distance coefficient and an R² of 0.3553, supporting the claim that geography matters but is incomplete.

## Figure 3 — `figures/final/final_east_southeast_asia_case_figure.png`

**Primary focused case: East/Southeast Asia.** This map focuses on Chinese, Japanese, Korean, Thai, Vietnamese, and Filipino cuisines. It is the strongest final inference case because the cuisine labels form a coherent regional/cross-subregional spatial set and the filtered residual results include strong positive links such as Thai–Vietnamese and Chinese–Korean. The figure supports spatial association, not a specific causal diffusion route.

## Figure 4 — `figures/final/final_geospatial_bridge_or_boundary_figure.png`

**Residual bridge scores.** Point size represents each cuisine’s long-distance positive residual bridge score. This is the strongest geospatial-only visual: it transforms pairwise cuisine-similarity residuals into mapped place-level roles. The result cannot be produced from ingredient vectors alone because it depends on residuals relative to distance, coordinates, and spatial pairing.

## Figure 5 — `figures/final/final_secondary_or_sensitivity_figure.png`

**Secondary/diagnostic case: Iberian/Atlantic-Pacific corridor.** Lines show residual links among Spanish, Brazilian, Mexican, Filipino, Cajun/Creole, Jamaican, and Southern U.S. cuisines. This case illustrates the corridor hypothesis vividly, especially for long-distance links, but should be interpreted cautiously because it is more vulnerable to English-language recipe-platform bias, colonial-history overinterpretation, and coarse cuisine-to-place mapping.

## Appendix Figure — `figures/final/final_boundary_permeability_appendix_figure.png`

**Boundary/permeability proxy.** This chart compares mean residuals by spatial boundary class, including same-subregion pairs, same-region/cross-subregion pairs, East/Southeast Asia cross-subregion pairs, Iberian/Atlantic interregional pairs, and other cross-region pairs. It supports the idea that residual strength varies by spatial grouping, but it remains exploratory and should motivate rather than replace stronger boundary or route modeling.
