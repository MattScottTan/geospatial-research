# Expanded StoryMap Callouts

## Research question

> When does cuisine similarity follow geographic distance, and when does it follow corridors, bridges, regions, or boundaries instead?

## Method

> A cuisine pair becomes a candidate corridor only after ingredient similarity is compared with geographic distance.

## Key finding

> Cuisine similarity is spatially structured, but not reducible to distance.

## Caution

> The project identifies candidate culinary corridors; it does not prove migration, trade, colonialism, maritime routes, or terrain causality.

## Geospatial contribution

> Ingredient data can identify resemblance; GIS identifies spatially unexpected resemblance and bridge roles.

## Final takeaway

> GIS transforms cuisine similarity from a list of shared ingredients into a map of residual corridors, bridges, and spatial interpretation.
