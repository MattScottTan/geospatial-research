I am preparing a Fisher Prize GIS project called **Culinary Corridors: Mapping Where Food Similarity Breaks Distance**. The project represents cuisines as ingredient profiles, compares cuisine similarity to geographic distance, and maps residual similarities as candidate culinary corridors. I would be grateful for your feedback on the food-science assumptions, especially the ingredient normalization, generic-ingredient filtering, and whether the interpretation stays appropriately cautious.

I have attached a one-page summary, figure list, and targeted questions. I am not asking for a full review of the GIS code; the most valuable feedback would be on whether the food-science framing is credible and what claims I should avoid.

Thank you,
Matthew

## One-page summary
The project asks when cuisine similarity follows geographic distance and when it breaks distance-based expectation. It uses a recipe/ingredient corpus to build cuisine profiles, computes similarity, fits a distance baseline, and maps positive residuals as candidate culinary corridors. The final story is global discovery plus East/Southeast Asia as the strongest focused case. The project avoids causal claims about migration or trade unless directly modeled.
