# EIP Case-Study Matrix

Selected from the project atlas and priority outputs.

## Selected cities

| Quadrant / role | City | Atlas signal | Why it works for EIP |
|---|---|---|---|
| Near compute / high AI | Singapore | 1,072 recent AI works; 4.1 km to nearest cloud region; access score 99.79 | Clear example of infrastructure, connectivity, policy, and research strength all aligning. |
| Near compute / low AI (in current overlay) | Seoul | 10 recent AI works; 1.3 km to nearest cloud region; access score 99.94 | Useful exception case showing that the current research overlay is narrower than the broader AI economy. |
| Far compute / high AI | Ho Chi Minh City | 373 recent AI works; 1,096.8 km to nearest cloud region; access score 57.79 | Strong “beats-the-pattern” case: meaningful AI activity despite weaker hyperscaler proximity. |
| Far compute / low AI / priority city | Lagos | 0 observed recent AI works in the delivered overlay; 3,842.6 km to nearest cloud region; access score 14.64 | Strong stacked-disadvantage case: huge urban market, but compute distance, power, and infrastructure frictions remain salient. |

## Cities considered but not selected

- **Bangkok**: looked attractive as a Southeast Asia low-access case, but current provider footprints have changed fast enough that it is no longer a clean “far compute” example.
- **Mexico City**: also attractive, but current hyperscaler footprints in Mexico have changed materially since the atlas snapshot.
- **Milan / Zurich**: likely better read as proxy-limit or matching-artifact cases than as headline public-interest stories.
- **Bangalore**: analytically useful, but less intuitive as a “far compute” case for a lay audience because its nearest-region distance is much smaller than Ho Chi Minh City’s.

## Intended narrative function

1. **Singapore** shows what a full infrastructure bundle looks like when it aligns.
2. **Seoul** shows that compute is not destiny and that the current OpenAlex research overlay is not the whole AI economy.
3. **Ho Chi Minh City** shows that policy, universities, and firms can produce meaningful AI activity even when local hyperscaler access is weaker.
4. **Lagos** shows stacked disadvantage and why infrastructure gaps still matter in fast-growing urban markets.

## Core EIP claim supported by the matrix

Compute is not destiny, but it is part of the infrastructure bundle that shapes AI opportunity. The case studies explain why some cities overperform or underperform relative to the atlas pattern by bringing in omitted variables that the regression cannot settle on its own.
