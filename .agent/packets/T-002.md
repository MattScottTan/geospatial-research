# Task

ID: T-002
Title: Paper 1 evidence — disconnection prevalence at scale
Objective: Determine empirically how often k-nearest-neighbour spatial weights matrices
silently disconnect on real geography, and what that does to Getis-Ord Gi\* hot/cold
classification.

# Relevant Acceptance Criteria

- **AC-006** Paper 1 establishes disconnection prevalence empirically across a range of
  sample sizes and `k` values drawn from real geography, reporting the fraction of
  configurations that silently disconnect.
- **AC-002** Every quantitative claim is traceable to a committed script and output field;
  re-execution reproduces it.

# Dependencies

None. All inputs exist.

# Relevant Decisions

- **D-002** Evidence is completed before prose is written. Paper 1's entire framing depends
  on your result: if disconnection turns out to be rare, the paper is reframed around the
  conditions that produce it. **Do not tune the experiment toward a desired answer.** A
  finding of "rare" is a perfectly good result and must be reported as such.
- **D-009** Null and negative results are reported with equal prominence.

# Constraints

- Reuse `tools/` and `code/spatialrmt/`. Do not fork or duplicate them.
- Explicit seed; deterministic re-runs.
- Sample real geography, not synthetic points — the claim is about actual practice.
- Sweep both `n` and `k`. Suggested but not binding: n ∈ {100, 200, 319, 500, 1000, 2000,

- Record per configuration at minimum: number of components, largest-component share,

  global and within-component standardisation.
